## 1. Overview

Dubai's archaeological record spans more than **300,000 years** of continuous human presence, from Lower Palaeolithic camps to late Islamic villages. Within that sequence, the **Bronze Age and early Iron Age (c. 3200–550 BC)** is currently the most actively researched period, yielding large cemeteries, industrial metal-working sites, and evidence of long-distance trade networks connecting the southeastern Arabian Peninsula with Mesopotamia, the Indus Valley, and the Iranian plateau.

More than **17 significant archaeological sites** have been identified across the emirate; all are managed by the **Dubai Culture and Arts Authority (Dubai Culture)**.

## 2. Key Sites

### 2.1 Saruq al-Hadid (c. 2600–550 BC)

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Discovery** | 2002, by H.H. Sheikh Mohammed bin Rashid Al Maktoum during an aerial survey |
| **Location** | Southern boundary of Dubai, fringe of the Rub' al-Khali (Empty Quarter) |
| **Period** | Late Bronze Age through Iron Age |
| **Significance** | One of the most important sites in southeastern Arabia; principal centre for **Iron Age metal smelting** |
| **Artefacts recovered** | Bronze & iron weapons (daggers, swords, axes, arrows), pottery & stone vessels, gold & silver pieces, thousands of beads (precious/semi-precious stone), decorative shells, local and foreign cylinder seals |
| **Geological setting** | Low dunes, exposed Miocene sandstone ridges, Quaternary deposits; gypsum crust ~100 m above sea level helps preserve sub-surface artefacts |

#### 2.1.1 AI-Assisted Remote Survey (2025)

A landmark **2025 study** led by **Khalifa University's Earth Sciences Department** in collaboration with **Sorbonne University Abu Dhabi** used cutting-edge remote-sensing technology to reveal the full extent of the site:

- **Technology stack:** L-band SAR data from the JAXA *ALOS-2* satellite (penetrates dry sand), high-resolution multispectral imagery from *WorldView-3*, and a bespoke AI pipeline combining ISODATA/K-means clustering, U-Net deep-learning segmentation, and terrain flow modelling.
- **Key findings:** Previously unrecorded archaeological features were detected buried under **up to 6 metres of sand**. Dense slag fields, circular arrangements consistent with kilns or wells, and rectangular wall-aligned forms were mapped across a much wider area than previously understood.
- **Ancient hydrology:** Historic stream channels — now completely buried — were reconstructed using elevation and slope data, confirming that the site had access to fresh water that supported sustained metallurgical production and possibly livestock.
- **Resolution:** Predictive model validated against known excavation zones; spatial accuracy to approximately **50 cm**.
- **Implication:** The scale of industrial activity at Saruq al-Hadid suggests organised production, advanced metallurgical knowledge, and participation in **long-distance trade networks** at a much earlier date than previously documented.

#### 2.1.2 Scholarly Publications — Saruq al-Hadid

- **"Bronze Age microliths at Saruq al-Hadid, Dubai"** — Moore et al., published in *Proceedings of the Seminar for Arabian Studies* (JSTOR stable: 27301203). Analyses backed microliths and trihedral points at the site, suggesting Neolithic/early Bronze Age human presence in the Rub' al-Khali fringe; also available via ResearchGate and Academia.edu.
- **"Neolithic arrowheads and Bronze Age industry at Saruq al Hadid, UAE"** — *Journal of Archaeological Science: Reports* (ScienceDirect DOI: 10.1016/j.jasrep.2024.002098, 2024). Introduces new bifacial arrowhead evidence and contextualises the site within growing proof of mid-Holocene coastal occupation in southern Arabia.

## 3. Civilisational Sequence in Bronze Age Dubai

| Period | Date Range | Diagnostic Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Hafit** | c. 3200–2600 BC | Beehive-shaped cairn tombs on hilltops; earliest Arabian Bronze Age |
| **Umm al-Nar** | c. 2600–2000 BC | Circular communal tombs; copper smelting; trade with Mesopotamia & Indus |
| **Wadi Suq** | c. 2000–1300 BC | Bronze weapons, softstone vessels; shift toward individual burials |
| **Late Bronze / Iron Age** | c. 1300–550 BC | Iron tools; continued metal smelting; falaj irrigation systems |

## 4. Methodological Advances

### 4.1 Remote Sensing & AI (2025 — Khalifa University / Sorbonne)
- SAR satellite penetration of desert sand for sub-surface mapping.
- U-Net deep-learning segmentation to distinguish cultural features from natural dune morphology.
- Predictive archaeological potential modelling via terrain and spectral analysis.
- Historic watercourse reconstruction using digital elevation models.

### 4.2 Traditional Excavation
- Stratigraphy-based chronology cross-referenced with Oman, Bahrain, and Iranian plateau sequences.
- Bioarchaeological analysis of skeletal remains (isotope and DNA studies ongoing).
- Radiocarbon and thermoluminescence dating of ceramics and charcoal.

## 5. Broader Significance & Trade Networks

Bronze Age Dubai sat at the intersection of several major ancient trade routes:

- **Sea routes** northward to **Mesopotamia** (modern Iraq) via the Arabian Gulf — copper and bronze artefacts from Dubai sites show compositional matches with Gulf exchange goods.
- **Overland routes** eastward to the **Oman peninsula** (Magan/Magan copper sources).
- **Connections to the Indus Valley Civilisation** evidenced by cylinder seals and bead types at Saruq al-Hadid and Al Sufouh.
- The scale of metal smelting at Saruq al-Hadid implies surplus production for **export**, not merely local use.
